1. What the research tells us

3 minutes estimated · Explain why we observe, reflect, try and check again.

Your outcome

Explain why we observe, reflect, try and check again.

Estimated reading time; check at your own pace. This is developmental support, separate from qualifications and formal assessment.

Start with the child

Mentoring helps an adult coach make one useful change and notice what it does for the children. We use observation, reflection, specific feedback and another attempt. Intent over outcome is our coaching value: recognise a purposeful decision even when execution is unsuccessful. It is not an empirical feedback-effect claim.

Findings and limits

Raabe and colleagues (2019) reviewed interventions with PE teachers and youth-sport coaches. Their review suggests support can change adult behaviour; it does not validate our Hub or a particular visit length. Haugan and colleagues (2021) studied a Norwegian coach-mentoring programme. Coach and athlete perceptions did not consistently agree. This setting and self-report evidence cannot establish effects for grassroots U6–U8 children. Feeling more confident is useful information, but does not demonstrate better practice or player experience.

Our proposed application

Agree one coach behaviour, observe what happens, let the coach interpret it, and test an achievable change. Ask what would count as an unhelpful result. If it does not help, adapt the idea rather than blame the coach. Psychological safety here means making it possible to ask for help and discuss mistakes without humiliation; safeguarding duties still apply.

Optional mentor guidance

FIFA’s student-coach example illustrates a pre-brief, agreed observation and reflective feedback in a licence-assessment setting. We adapt the conversation for development, separate from formal assessment. A successful playing career does not replace preparation in listening, questioning, feedback and boundaries. Practise describing an event before offering advice.

Choose one coaching behaviour and describe what the children might experience differently.

2. Choosing a mentoring method

4 minutes estimated · Choose support that fits the coach and return ownership.

Your outcome

Choose support that fits the coach and return ownership.

Estimated reading time; check at your own pace. This is developmental support, separate from qualifications and formal assessment.

Four practical options

Ask and reflect: help the coach compare options. Observe and feed back: describe specific events rather than personality. Demonstrate and hand back: show one brief agreed example, then the coach tries. Plan or coach together: share a defined task and agree when the coach resumes it.

Make a simple agreement

Use “Watch me”, “Show me” or “Work with me”. Agree the purpose and hand-back point. A beginner who cannot picture a game may need a demonstration; questions alone can frustrate them. An experienced coach may want to test alternatives rather than receive a running commentary.

Illustrative volunteer example: engagement

A volunteer notices long waits. Agree to shorten the explanation and run two smaller games. Observe waiting in the same short window before and afterwards. Hand the organisation back to the volunteer. A busier session is not, by itself, proof of retained learning; ask about involvement and revisit the setup.

Optional mentor guidance

Ask permission before changing methods. “Would a short example help?” Explain what you will show, keep it brief and invite the coach’s own attempt immediately. Do not take over the entire practice by default.

Take it to the field

Agree Watch me, Show me or Work with me, including when ownership returns.

3. Before the visit

3 minutes estimated · Confirm the arrangement, one focus and supervision.

Your outcome

Confirm the arrangement, one focus and supervision.

Estimated reading time; check at your own pace. This is developmental support, separate from qualifications and formal assessment.

Reuse the request

Check the current season/team, age group, practice or game, time and location. Check who is currently assigned and whether they have accepted. A submitted form is requested, not booked. Cover for an absent coach is a separate staffing responsibility with eligibility checks.

Agree one focus

Ask: what would make coaching easier? Choose one observable adult behaviour and one thing the children might do differently. Agree Watch me, Show me or Work with me; when may the mentor intervene and when should feedback wait? Record the accepted arrangement before marking confirmed.

Responsibilities remain explicit

Name who retains team supervision, emergency responsibility and substitutions. Maintain never-alone and existing supervision requirements throughout demonstrations and adult debriefs. Mentoring does not grant team-area eligibility or replace required screening, qualifications or safeguarding training.

Optional mentor guidance

If time, venue, focus or mentor changes, reconfirm rather than assume. Do not import old spreadsheet rows as current appointments. Use the printable pre-visit agreement to check the details together.

Take it to the field

Agree one behaviour, one child-experience sign and a confirmed arrangement.

4. During practice

4 minutes estimated · Observe facts, support briefly and hand back.

Your outcome

Observe facts, support briefly and hand back.

Estimated reading time; check at your own pace. This is developmental support, separate from qualifications and formal assessment.

Observe first

Unless a demonstration was agreed, let the coach lead. Capture two factual examples: what the adult did and what the children did next, without identifying children. “The explanation lasted through two restarts” is more useful than “poor communicator”. Keep observation windows short enough to maintain your assigned duties.

Make one useful adjustment

Offer a brief question or demonstration, or adjust space, numbers or challenge. Return ownership. Avoid long queues and explanations. Never use exclusion, exercise or humiliation as punishment for mistakes. Act immediately on safety concerns through the applicable procedure; do not wait for a debrief.

Illustrative volunteer example: support and passing

Start with a manageable 4v1, add a second defender when appropriate, then revisit support in a wider game. The 4v1 progression comes from the supplied mentoring proposal; it is not a newly verified Hub resource. The existing 4v2 Rondo is tagged U12/U14, so adapt cautiously rather than prescribe it to beginners. Ask about another passing option instead of calling every pass. Observe whether teammates offer choices and the coach allows decision time.

Existing resources and boundaries

Use Hide & Seek for protecting and finding support, or a small 3v3 game adapted to the children. Hide & Seek and 4v2 Rondo are verified Hub activities; the particular progressive sequence is an illustrative application. Practice constraints and bonus scoring do not alter official match rules. Review old conditions before reuse; do not revive punitive removal rules.

Record two factual examples, one short intervention and the hand-back.

5. Supporting coaches during games

5 minutes estimated · Use the matchday card while the coach leads.

Your outcome

Use the matchday card while the coach leads.

Estimated reading time; check at your own pace. This is developmental support, separate from qualifications and formal assessment.

Before kick-off

Connect the match focus to practice. Agree what to observe, mentor location, quiet-prompt method and supervision duties. Choose whole-match support or a clearly timed window. Check local team-area eligibility, playing-time/substitution rules and emergency arrangements. A mentor without approved team-area access observes from a permitted location.

During play

The coach leads and the mentor observes. Offer a brief quiet prompt only as agreed. Do not issue competing player instructions, argue with spectators or officials, or enter the field without authorisation. Safety overrides waiting for a debrief. Support an agreed duty only if it does not compromise supervision.

Half-time

Water and recovery first. The coach leads. Share one useful observation and one achievable cue, with player input where appropriate. Recognise a purposeful attempt. Do not turn a short break into a second adult lecture or delay a restart.

After the final whistle

Finish supervision and safe handover before an adult debrief, or agree a later conversation. No public critique. Capture one learning observation and one next-practice action. The score provides context; it is not the measure of mentoring success.

Illustrative staff-coach example

Agree to observe tone, body language and intervention timing under pressure. After conceding, use the agreed quiet prompt. Recognise a brave decision even if possession was lost. Later compare one helpful intervention with a moment where silence would have helped. Keep this developmental conversation separate from formal performance assessment.

Practitioner guidance

England Football: managing matchday and creating a great matchday experience provide practical guidance on player involvement, observation and adult behaviour. These are practitioner resources, not trials establishing programme effectiveness. Use the four-section blank matchday card in My mentoring → Field cards.

6. After the visit and into the next session

3 minutes estimated · Agree one next action and check what happened.

Your outcome

Agree one next action and check what happened.

Estimated reading time; check at your own pace. This is developmental support, separate from qualifications and formal assessment.

Let the coach speak first

Ask what worked, what the children did and what the coach would change. Add one specific strength and one useful factual observation. Keep feedback private and about behaviour, not personality. The coach chooses one achievable next action.

Close the loop

Link one relevant activity or lesson and agree a follow-up owner/date. Ask whether the action was tried and what happened. “Not yet” and “It did not help” are valid responses. Decide whether to retry, adapt or close with a reason; do not manufacture success.

Protect supervision

A debrief waits until supervision and safe handover are complete. If time is short, agree a later adult conversation. A short practice visit and whole-match support require different time allocations; agree capacity instead of promising overlapping coverage.

Optional mentor guidance

At the next visit, revisit the previous action before introducing a new topic. Compare the same adult behaviour and a relevant sign of player experience. One positive observation is a starting point for enquiry, not proof of lasting learning.

7. Recording support and following through

3 minutes estimated · Keep useful adult records and respect their limits.

Your outcome

Keep useful adult records and respect their limits.

Estimated reading time; check at your own pace. This is developmental support, separate from qualifications and formal assessment.

Four different records

A request describes a need and proposed arrangement. Linked visits preserve each occasion separately. An action records the next attempt; a follow-up records what happened or why it closed. Mentor assignment does not establish attendance. Lesson completion, delivered support and improved practice are separate things.

Who sees the notes

These are shared adult development records linked to your account, not anonymous notes. The coach, currently assigned mentor and authorised programme administrator can see them. Ask for a correction through the same record or coordinator; changes retain author/time and field-change metadata. Do not promise absolute confidentiality or use this as formal assessment.

Keep children out of the log

Do not enter children’s names, identifying details, health information, private family information, safeguarding allegations or identifiable child footage. Record adult behaviours and non-identifying observations only. Safeguarding concerns go immediately through the approved restricted reporting procedure, never the mentoring queue. See AYSO Safe Haven and U.S. Soccer reporting guidance; the local reporting procedure must be confirmed before pilot collection.

Follow through honestly

Use requested, arranging, confirmed, delivered, follow-up due, closed, postponed or cancelled. Confirmation needs the coach’s agreement and current mentor’s acceptance. Delivery requires a coach/mentor confirmation the visit happened. A past date is not attendance. Delivery must preserve outstanding follow-ups. Close only when actions have results or closure reasons. Retention and deletion, including backups, need an approved organisational policy; no fixed period is assumed.

Check one shared adult record for accuracy and agree the next follow-up.
